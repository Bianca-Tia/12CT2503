Newton's Cradle
Who made it
The cradle grew out of several people's work. In 1662, John Wallis, Christopher Wren and Christiaan Huygens presented the principles of collisions to the Royal Society, and in the early 1670s the French physicist and priest Edme Mariotte recorded the first experiments with colliding pendulum balls. In 1967, the English actor Simon Prebble turned the idea into a desk toy and named it after Isaac Newton.
When
1662 to the early 1670s: the collision principles are worked out and tested.
1687: Newton publishes his laws of motion, which explain them.
1967: the desk toy is named and sold through Harrods in London.
Why it was made
  • To study how motion passes from one body to another in a collision.
  • As a desk toy, named after Newton because his laws explain it.
First materials
  • 1967 version: wooden frame.
  • Today: polished steel balls, two strings each.
What it explains
  • Conservation of momentum.
  • Conservation of energy in nearly elastic collisions.
  • One ball in, one ball out; two in, two out.
What gets solved for
Momentum p = m·v
Kinetic energy KE = ½·m·v²
Potential energy PE = m·g·h, where h = L(1 − cos θ)
Velocity v = p ÷ m
Sources: HowStuffWorks, ScienceABC, UCSB Physics Lecture Demonstrations.
